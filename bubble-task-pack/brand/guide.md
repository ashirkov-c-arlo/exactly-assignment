# Bubble illustration guide

This is the active Bubble illustration guidance for this exercise. Rule IDs are stable citation IDs.
The approved references at the end show how the rules work in practice; they do not turn every
repeated visual detail into a rule.

## BRAND-01 — Clear, capable, warm

Bubble illustrations should feel approachable, energetic, professional, and slightly playful.
People should look capable and engaged. Humor may come from an exaggerated prop or situation, but
not from ridicule, distress, or caricature.

The requested action should be understandable without facial expression or explanatory text.

## STYLE-01 — Flat matte surfaces

Use flat color fills with crisp edges. Do not use gradients, lighting, cast shadows, glow, gloss,
grain, brush texture, or modeled 3D volume.

A single darker flat side plane is allowed when an object needs simple depth. The boundary must stay
hard; it should not read as shading.

This is a blocking rule when rendered lighting, texture, or 3D treatment becomes a visible part of
the image.

## STYLE-02 — Shapes before outlines

Build people and objects from a few bold filled silhouettes. Adjacent fills and white gaps should
define most boundaries. Universal black outlines, sketch contours, and dense line detail are outside
the style.

Thin functional lines are allowed for parts that cannot be shown clearly as masses: cables, handles,
stems, headset bands, spokes, whisk wires, and similar hardware. A complex object may need more
parts, but it should still feel compatible with the simplified character.

## COLOR-01 — Restricted palette

The working palette is black, white, signature red, and a small hierarchy of neutral grays:

| Role | Anchor |
|---|---:|
| Signature red | `#D71616` |
| Black | `#000000` |
| Charcoal | `#2D2E2F` |
| Medium gray | `#898A8B` |
| Light gray | `#C8C9CA` |
| Pale gray | `#E3E3E3` |
| White | `#FFFFFF` |

Minor production variation is acceptable. Judge whether colors keep their visual role rather than
requiring an exact pixel match. Blues, greens, purples, realistic skin colors, decorative pastel
palettes, and colored lighting are not approved.

## COLOR-02 — Red carries meaning

Visible skin is normally red: face, ears, hands, and exposed forearms. Red may also mark the main
interaction or symbol. It should remain concentrated rather than spread evenly through the scene.

Large red fields are appropriate when the red object carries the message, such as a flag, calendar
header, confirmation panel, or tool body. Large trouser-like red leg shapes are not approved because
they read as exposed skin and weaken the clothing structure.

## COLOR-03 — Yellow is a rare exception

Golden yellow (`#FFBA0A`) is reserved for a concept-specific accent such as a key or flowers. Do not
use it as a routine second brand color.

## CHAR-01 — Modular characters

Use a small head, compact torso, thick curved limbs, simplified mitten-like hands, and detached
shoe-like shapes. Hair is a clean black mass without strands or highlights. Faces are featureless or
reduced to a simple profile silhouette; eyes and expressive mouths are normally absent.

Hands are normally red. A clearly intentional work glove may be gray. Small finger separation is
allowed when a specific gesture needs it, but realistic hand anatomy is not.

Detached hands or shoes are acceptable when the pose remains easy to read. Do not flag detachment by
itself.

## CHAR-02 — Exaggeration must remain coherent

Bubble anatomy is intentionally elastic. Legs may be long, limbs may bend strongly, and realistic
proportion is not required. Exaggeration becomes a problem when limb ownership, clothing, balance,
or the requested action becomes hard to understand.

Do not reject an image merely because a limb is longer than a realistic limb. Describe the concrete
reading problem instead.

## POSE-01 — The pose explains the action

Body language is the main carrier of meaning. Head direction, hand placement, weight, and limb flow
should support the requested action. A viewer should not have to guess whether a person is sitting,
holding, carrying, reaching, or interacting with a prop.

An unusual pose is not automatically wrong. If the intended action is still plausible but important
body relationships are ambiguous, send the image to human review rather than inventing a defect.

## COMP-01 — One visual idea

Each image should have one dominant action or focal cluster. Use a few large masses, generous white
space, and at most a small amount of pale contextual scenery. Avoid a fully described room, complex
perspective, or several competing stories.

A bust or partial figure is allowed when the crop feels intentional and the requested action remains
clear.

Black should anchor the composition, gray should support it, and red should guide attention.

## PROP-01 — Simplify and scale for meaning

Reduce props to the fewest shapes needed for recognition. A meaningful object may be much larger
than realistic scale: keys, parcels, signs, phones, tools, and furniture can dominate the scene.
Oversized scale is approved when the action remains clear.

Functional equipment may use more structural detail than a person, but avoid realistic mechanical
rendering, intricate controls, and line-heavy perspective that makes it look drawn in a different
style.

## COPY-01 — Follow the request exactly

Signs, screens, and panels should usually remain blank when no copy is requested. When the request
requires visible wording, the wording must be present and materially correct. Treat copy as a clean
layout element; do not use decorative hand-lettering or pseudo-text.

## GOV-01 — Rules, requests, and references

The active rules above define Bubble policy. The short request defines what the particular image is
meant to show and may add content requirements. It cannot waive an active rule.

Approved references demonstrate accepted outcomes and boundary variations. Use them as evidence,
not as an exhaustive checklist. When an active rule and an approved example appear to conflict,
return `human_review` and explain the conflict.
